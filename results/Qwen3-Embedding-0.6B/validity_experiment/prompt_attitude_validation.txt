You are an expert annotator of policy texts. Your task is to construct an independent validation set for measuring **policy-attitude intensity differences**.

You will receive 4,000 pairs of Chinese policy-text segments. Each observation contains:

* `sample_id`: the unique identifier of the observation;
* `query`: the reference policy-text segment;
* `direct_match`: the matched policy-text segment.

You must complete the task in **two sequential stages**:

**Stage 1: Select exactly 500 observations that exhibit the clearest, strongest, and most unambiguous NON-ZERO differences in policy-attitude intensity.**

**Stage 2: For those 500 selected observations only, classify the attitude intensity of `direct_match` relative to `query` using the coding scheme specified below.**

IMPORTANT: The purpose of Stage 1 is specifically to identify observations that DISPLAY A MEANINGFUL ATTITUDE-INTENSITY DIFFERENCE. Observations whose correct label would be `0` MUST NOT be selected, even if the absence of a difference is exceptionally clear.

Do not output any intermediate reasoning, scores, candidate lists, explanations, or textual comments. Perform the reasoning internally and return only the final 500 annotations.

### 1. Definition of policy-attitude intensity

In this task, **policy attitude** refers to the intensity with which policymakers treat, prioritize, require, promote, or pursue a policy task. It reflects the decision maker's stance regarding the **priority, urgency, commitment, or strength of implementation** attached to a policy task.

Policy-attitude intensity may be expressed through, for example:

* differences in obligation or requirement;
* differences in urgency;
* differences in the strength of policy action;
* differences in prioritization;
* differences in commitment;
* degree adverbs or intensifying/mitigating expressions;
* verbs that imply different strengths of intervention, implementation, promotion, restriction, support, or enforcement.

Policy-attitude intensity is **not**:

* general semantic similarity between the two texts;
* positive versus negative sentiment;
* whether a policy objective is normatively desirable;
* the importance of the substantive policy domain itself;
* the factual scale or economic importance of the policy target;
* your own evaluation of the policy.

Judge only what is expressed linguistically in the two policy-text segments.

### 2. Comparison direction and coding scheme

All labels describe the policy-attitude intensity of **`direct_match` relative to `query`**.

Use the following coding scheme:

* `2`: `direct_match` is **clearly stronger** in policy-attitude intensity than `query`;
* `1`: `direct_match` is **slightly but meaningfully stronger** than `query`;
* `0`: there is **no meaningful difference** in policy-attitude intensity;
* `-1`: `direct_match` is **slightly but meaningfully weaker** than `query`;
* `-2`: `direct_match` is **clearly weaker** than `query`.

The direction must never be reversed.

The `0` category remains part of the general coding scheme for conceptual completeness, but **it is an exclusion category for Stage 1**. Because Stage 1 selects observations specifically for displaying attitude-intensity differences, no observation judged to have label `0` may appear in the final 500 observations.

### 3. Stage 1: selecting the 500 clearest NON-ZERO observations

Before assigning the final labels, independently inspect all 4,000 observations.

First determine whether each pair contains a **meaningful directional difference in policy-attitude intensity**.

For Stage 1, divide observations conceptually into:

**Eligible observations**
The comparison supports one of the directional labels:

`{-2, -1, 1, 2}`

**Ineligible observations**
The comparison supports:

`0`

or the direction of the difference cannot be determined reliably.

Only eligible observations may be considered for the final 500.

A pair for which "there is clearly no difference" is NOT a good Stage-1 candidate. Clarity of equality must not be confused with clarity of difference.

Among eligible observations, select exactly **500 observations with the clearest and most salient attitude-intensity differences**.

Give priority to observations satisfying the following conditions:

1. `query` and `direct_match` express sufficiently comparable policy tasks, actions, or orientations;
2. there is a genuine non-zero difference in policy-attitude intensity;
3. the relevant attitude-bearing expressions can be clearly identified;
4. the direction of the difference is linguistically explicit;
5. the judgment does not require external knowledge;
6. the direction is unlikely to change under a reasonable alternative reading;
7. the intensity contrast itself, rather than a substantive difference in policy content, explains the comparison.

Deprioritize or exclude observations in which:

* the appropriate label would be `0`;
* the texts differ semantically but not in policy-attitude intensity;
* the two texts concern substantially different policy actions or objects;
* one or both texts contain several clauses with conflicting levels of intensity;
* the comparison depends heavily on missing surrounding context;
* negation or complex logical structure makes the direction ambiguous;
* the difference is primarily substantive rather than attitudinal;
* the relative intensity cannot be judged confidently.

For internal selection, classify NON-ZERO candidates by confidence and salience:

* **Tier A:** highly explicit, strong, and unambiguous directional intensity difference;
* **Tier B:** clear and meaningful directional intensity difference, but smaller in magnitude or requiring modest semantic interpretation;
* **Tier C:** weak, ambiguous, context-dependent, or disputable directional difference.

Select Tier A observations before Tier B observations. Avoid Tier C whenever enough Tier A and Tier B observations are available.

Within equally reliable candidates, prefer observations with **larger and more explicit attitude-intensity contrasts**. Thus, all else being equal, an observation that clearly warrants `2` or `-2` should be preferred over one that warrants only `1` or `-1`.

However, do not artificially assign `2` or `-2` merely to make a candidate appear stronger. Label magnitude must still reflect the actual linguistic contrast.

When two observations are otherwise equally suitable for inclusion, apply the following deterministic tie-breaking rules:

1. prefer the observation with the stronger and more explicit linguistic indication of intensity difference;
2. then prefer the observation with the clearer comparability of the policy task;
3. then prefer the observation requiring less contextual inference;
4. if they remain indistinguishable, select the observation with the smaller `sample_id`.

You must select **exactly 500 unique sample IDs**.

Before proceeding to Stage 2, internally verify:

* exactly 500 observations have been selected;
* every selected observation contains a meaningful directional attitude-intensity difference;
* none of the selected observations would receive label `0`.

### 4. Stage 2: labeling the selected observations

After the 500 observations have been fixed, independently determine the attitude-intensity difference for each selected pair.

Remove that observation from the selected set and replace it with the next-highest-ranked eligible NON-ZERO observation from Stage 1. Repeat this verification until all 500 selected observations have non-zero labels.

Do not use or attempt to infer any external numerical similarity score, dictionary score, or pre-existing label. Your annotation must be based exclusively on the textual contents of `query` and `direct_match`.
